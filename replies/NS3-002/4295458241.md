Aarohi, as promised, here is the DSDV version of the two-run comparison. Thank you again for the review that prompted it.

We gave every node three active interfaces: its Wi-Fi interface and two wired links in a ring. We ran stock DSDV once alone and once after a run that reshapes the heap, with the same seed, in the same process. We did this on `master` `a150de8f` and on ns-3.49, three times each.

- The send order moves with the heap. Alone, every node sends on interfaces `1 2 3`. After the heap-churning run, 7 of 8 nodes send in another order.
- The results do not move. Every DSDV result row is identical in every run, while the AODV case in the same runs still differs.

So, on this topology, DSDV's results do not depend on the heap, consistent with your observation that DSDV draws no random number inside its send loops. We have not shown that no topology makes them depend on it. Keying the maps by interface index would still make the order deterministic, and for DSDV's `NotifyRemoveAddress()` it is the variant that works.

The details are at https://github.com/swarm-routing/sim-tools/issues/301#issuecomment-7088105942, and the test is at https://github.com/swarm-routing/sim-tools/pull/306. With DSDV's default buffering, this topology also hits the separate DSDV bug of nsnam/ns-3-dev#1364, so the test turns buffering off.
